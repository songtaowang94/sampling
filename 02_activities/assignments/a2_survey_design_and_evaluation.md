## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. Sample type: Probabilistic sample. The survey utilized a cross-sectional design.

2. Sample size:** Approximately 16,149 respondents (data collected from this number of individuals out of the initial sample).

3. Target population:** All non-institutionalized persons aged 15 years and older, living in the 10 provinces of Canada. It excludes full-time residents of institutions and residents of the Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut.

4. Sampling frame:** The 2018 GVP used the "Dwelling Universe Frame" (derived from the Address Register) in major urban centers and a telephone frame (landline and cellular numbers) for other areas, depending on the specific phase of the sampling design overlap with the Labour Force Survey or direct RDD (Random Digit Dialing) methods utilized by GSS. Note: GSS methodology transitioned during these cycles, but it primarily relies on StatsCan's centralized frame services.

5. Survey mode(s):** Mixed-mode collection: Computer Assisted Telephone Interviewing (CATI) and Electronic Questionnaire (EQ/Web).

6. Timeline:** Data collection took place from September 2018 to December 2018.

7. Response rate:** The overall response rate was 48.8%.

8. Weights:** The survey provides a master survey weight (WTPP) which indicates the number of people in the population that the respondent represents. Bootstrap weights are also provided for estimating variance and calculating the coefficient of variation.

9. Data processing:** Data capture occurred directly via CATI/EQ systems. Processing involved coding of open-ended questions (e.g., occupation, industry, volunteering types) and classification based on standard codes (NAICS, NOC).

10. Cleaning, imputation, etc.:** Consistency edits were run to check for logical errors (e.g., a person claiming to volunteer more hours than exist in a week). Imputation was used for item non-response (missing values) in specific key variables, often using "nearest neighbour" donor imputation.

11. Sources of error:** * Sampling error: The error arising because a sample was used rather than a census (measured by standard error/CV).
 Non-sampling error: Coverage error (exclusions of territories/homeless), Non-response error (refusal to participate), and Measurement error (misinterpretation of questions).

12. Limitations, known biases, etc.:** * **Recall Bias:** As the survey asks about volunteering and charitable giving over the *past 12 months*, respondents may forget small donations or informal volunteering, leading to under-reporting.
Social Desirability Bias:** Respondents may over-report their giving and volunteering behaviors to appear more altruistic to the interviewer.
Under-coverage:** The exclusion of the territories and institutional residents means the data is not fully representative of the entire Canadian nation.

13. Link to documentation:** 
https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=4430
```
